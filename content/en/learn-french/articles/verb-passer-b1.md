# Idioms and fixed expressions with *passer*

At [A2](/en/cefr/), *passer* covers going past, spending time, and stopping by. That core is covered in the [French Verb of the Week lesson on *passer*](/en/learn-french/votw/votw-passer-a2/).

Around [B1](/en/cefr/), prepositions and pronominal forms reshape the verb. Learn those constructions as wholes rather than hunting for another translation of *passer*.

## What happened? *se passer*

On its own, the pronominal form asks or explains what happened.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ? | What happened? |
| Il s'est passé quelque chose ? | Did something happen? |
| Rien ne s'est passé. | Nothing happened. |

This is one of the most common spoken uses of *passer* beyond the A2 senses.

## Going without: *se passer de*

*Se passer de* means managing without something.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je ne peux pas me passer de café. | I can't do without coffee. |
| Elle se passe facilement de voiture. | She manages without a car. |
| Nous avons dû nous passer d'Internet. | We had to do without the Internet. |

Nothing is "passing" here. Life continues without the thing named after *de*.

## Being taken for: *passer pour*

With *pour*, *passer* means being considered or having a reputation.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Il passe pour un expert. | He's considered an expert. |
| Elle passe pour quelqu'un de sérieux. | She's regarded as a serious person. |

*Passer pour* is about other people's opinion, not your own claim.

## Change, notice, and action

Several fixed expressions describe change over time, or a move into a new stage.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Il est passé de débutant à expert. | He went from beginner to expert. |
| Son erreur est passée inaperçue. | His mistake went unnoticed. |
| Il faut passer à l'action. | We need to take action. |

English changes verbs (*go*, *go unnoticed*, *take*). French keeps *passer*.

## A pattern worth noticing

Past the everyday [A2](/en/cefr/) meanings, *passer* often depends on what follows:

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| *se passer* | happen |
| *se passer de* | do without |
| *passer pour* | be regarded as |
| *passer de… à…* | go from… to… |
| *passer inaperçu* | go unnoticed |
| *passer à l'action* | take action |

These are better learned as complete expressions than as separate dictionary senses.

## Summary

Beyond the everyday uses, *passer* becomes a verb of fixed constructions. Recognizing those patterns will make conversations and news French easier to follow at [B1](/en/cefr/).
