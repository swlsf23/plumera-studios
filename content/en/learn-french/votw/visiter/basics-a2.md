# Visiter - basics

English *visit* covers museums and friends with the same verb. French splits the job. You *visitez* a museum. You *rendez visite* to a friend.

That split is the whole lesson for many English speakers. *Visiter* looks safe because it resembles *visit*, and for places it is. For people in ordinary social situations, French usually reaches for another pattern: *rendre visite à*.

The shared idea behind *visiter* is going through a place to see it or check it: a city, a monument, an apartment you might rent. A person is not that kind of place. Keep the split in mind, and lines like *visiter un musée* next to *rendre visite à quelqu'un* stop feeling random.

## Forms and grammar

*Visiter* is a regular *-er* verb in the present tense, so the forms are steady once you know the pattern.

<!-- table: forms -->

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| je visite | nous visitons |
| tu visites | vous visitez |
| il / elle visite | ils / elles visitent |

In the past tense, *visiter* uses *avoir*. The past participle is *visité*.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| J'ai visité le Louvre hier. | I visited the Louvre yesterday. |
| Nous avons visité Lyon en mai. | We visited Lyon in May. |

## How to use visiter

The uses below move from sightseeing to looking a place over carefully. The people contrast sits next to the first sense, because that is where English speakers usually go wrong.

### Visiting a place

To go around a place and see it: a city, a museum, a monument, a park.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Nous visitons le musée demain. | We're visiting the museum tomorrow. |
| Elle a visité Rome l'année dernière. | She visited Rome last year. |
| Tu veux visiter le château ? | Do you want to visit the castle? |

For people in everyday social life, French uses *rendre visite à*, not *visiter*.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je rends visite à mes grands-parents. | I'm visiting my grandparents. |
| Elle a rendu visite à son ami. | She visited her friend. |
| Nous allons rendre visite à Léa. | We're going to visit Léa. |

*Rendre visite* takes *à* before the person. The verb that changes is *rendre*, and *visite* stays in that fixed shape. For more on *rendre* itself, see the [Verb of the Week lesson on *rendre*](/en/learn-french/votw/rendre/basics-a2/).

### Looking a place over

To go through a place carefully, often to check it before a decision, such as renting or buying.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Nous visitons un appartement samedi. | We're viewing an apartment on Saturday. |
| Ils ont visité trois maisons. | They looked at three houses. |
| Je visite le studio cet après-midi. | I'm viewing the studio this afternoon. |

Here you are still dealing with a place. The focus is inspection more than tourism.

### Visiting a website

To open and look at a site online. French keeps *visiter* for this too.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Tu as visité leur site ? | Have you visited their site? |
| Visitez notre page pour plus d'infos. | Visit our page for more information. |
| J'ai visité le site de la mairie. | I visited the town hall website. |

## Other common constructions

Three combinations that sit next to *visiter* when you talk about people or guided tours:

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| rendre visite à | to visit (someone) |
| aller voir | to go see (someone) |
| une visite guidée | a guided tour |

*Aller voir* is the everyday spoken choice for seeing friends or family. *Rendre visite à* is clear and neutral. *Une visite guidée* keeps the noun *visite* with places: the tour is of a place, not a person.

## Related verbs

- rendre: to give back, and in *rendre visite à*, to visit someone
- aller: to go
- passer: to stop by
- découvrir: to discover

> *Rendre* is the one to watch next to *visiter*. English keeps one verb for museums and friends. French uses *visiter* for the museum and *rendre visite à* for the friend.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je visite un musée. | I'm visiting a museum. |
| Je rends visite à un ami. | I'm visiting a friend. |

## Before you go

Think of *visiter* as going through a place to see or check it. At [A2](/en/cefr/), that covers museums, cities, apartments, and websites, while everyday visits to people usually take *rendre visite à* or *aller voir*.

For the wider verb *rendre*, including why *rendre visite* fits its pattern, see the [Verb of the Week lesson on *rendre*](/en/learn-french/votw/rendre/basics-a2/).
