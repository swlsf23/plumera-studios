# Idioms and fixed expressions with *passer*

At [A2](/en/cefr/), *passer* covers passing by, spending time, and stopping somewhere. Around [B1](/en/cefr/), you begin meeting expressions where the preposition or the pronominal form carries most of the meaning. Learn these as complete patterns rather than trying to translate *passer* on its own.

## Going without: *se passer de*

This pronominal construction means managing without something.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je ne peux pas me passer de café. | I can't do without coffee. |
| Elle se passe facilement de voiture. | She manages without a car. |
| Nous avons dû nous passer d'Internet. | We had to do without the Internet. |

The idea is not that something "passes." It is that life continues without it.

## What happened? *se passer*

On its own, the pronominal form asks or explains what happened.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ? | What happened? |
| Il s'est passé quelque chose ? | Did something happen? |
| Rien ne s'est passé. | Nothing happened. |

This is one of the most common spoken uses of *passer*.

## Being taken for: *passer pour*

With *pour*, *passer* means being considered or having a reputation.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Il passe pour un expert. | He's considered an expert. |
| Elle passe pour quelqu'un de sérieux. | She's regarded as a serious person. |

*Passer pour* is about other people's opinion, not your own.

## Time and change

Several fixed expressions describe change over time.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| passer de... à... | to go from... to... |
| passer inaperçu | to go unnoticed |
| passer à l'action | to take action |

Notice how English changes verbs while French keeps *passer*.

## A pattern worth noticing

Past the core A2 meanings, *passer* often depends on the word that follows:

- *se passer de* (do without)
- *se passer* (happen)
- *passer pour* (be regarded as)
- *passer de... à...* (go from... to...)

These are better learned as complete expressions than as separate meanings of the verb.

## Summary

Beyond the everyday uses, *passer* becomes a verb of fixed constructions. Recognising these patterns will make conversations and news articles much easier to follow at [B1](/en/cefr/).
