# Idioms and fixed expressions with *laisser*

At [A2](/en/cefr/), *laisser* covers leaving things behind and letting someone do something. Around [B1](/en/cefr/), you begin meeting pronominal forms and fixed expressions where the meaning is carried by the whole construction rather than the verb alone.

## Letting yourself...

The pronominal form *se laisser* means allowing something to happen to yourself, either deliberately or passively.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Elle s'est laissée convaincre. | She let herself be convinced. |
| Ne te laisse pas décourager. | Don't let yourself get discouraged. |
| Il se laisse facilement influencer. | He lets himself be influenced easily. |

English often changes the verb completely. French keeps *se laisser*.

## Letting something happen

*Laisser* followed by an infinitive can express allowing events to unfold without interference.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Laisse passer quelques jours. | Let a few days go by. |
| Il a laissé tomber la discussion. | He dropped the discussion. |
| Laissons faire le temps. | Let's let time do its work. |

Notice that *laisser tomber* often means abandoning an idea rather than physically dropping an object.

## Fixed expressions

Several everyday expressions are difficult to understand word for word.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| se laisser faire | to let yourself be pushed around |
| laisser entendre que | to imply that |
| laisser une bonne impression | to leave a good impression |

The meaning belongs to the whole phrase, not to *laisser* by itself.

## Literary and formal uses

In writing and more formal speech, *laisser* often introduces a result or impression.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Cette décision laisse penser que... | This decision suggests that... |
| Son attitude laisse croire qu'il est prêt. | His attitude suggests that he's ready. |

French often uses *laisser* where English prefers *suggest* or *make someone think*.

## A pattern worth noticing

Beyond the everyday A2 meanings, *laisser* often appears in larger constructions:

- *se laisser* + infinitive
- *laisser tomber*
- *laisser entendre que*
- *laisser penser que*

As you reach B1, it is more useful to recognise these patterns than to search for another translation of *laisser*.

## Summary

Past the core meanings of leaving and allowing, *laisser* becomes a verb of implication, permission, and attitude. Learning these expressions as complete units will make them much easier to recognise in authentic French.
