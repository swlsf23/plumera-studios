# Idioms and fixed expressions with *rendre*

At [A2](/en/cefr/), *rendre* covers giving something back, handing work in, and making someone become a certain way. Around [B1](/en/cefr/), you begin meeting pronominal forms and fixed constructions where the meaning belongs to the whole expression rather than the verb alone.

## Realising something: *se rendre compte de*

This is one of the most useful pronominal expressions in French.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je me rends compte de mon erreur. | I realise my mistake. |
| Tu te rends compte ? | Can you believe it? |
| Nous nous sommes rendu compte du problème trop tard. | We realised the problem too late. |

Learn *se rendre compte de* as a complete unit. It does not mean "to give oneself an account."

## Going or surrendering: *se rendre à*

With *à*, *se rendre* often means going somewhere, especially in formal French. In other contexts, it means surrendering.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Le président s'est rendu à Bruxelles. | The president went to Brussels. |
| Ils se sont rendus sur place. | They went to the scene. |
| Les soldats se sont rendus. | The soldiers surrendered. |

The surrounding context usually makes the meaning clear.

## Rendering and providing

Several fixed expressions use *rendre* where English changes the verb completely.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| rendre hommage à | to pay tribute to |
| rendre service à | to do someone a favour |
| rendre justice à | to do justice to |

The shared idea is giving something that is due, whether respect, help, or fairness.

## Reporting and accounting

French uses *rendre compte* in several related expressions.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| rendre compte de | to report on |
| rendre des comptes | to be accountable |
| compte rendu | report, written account |

Notice that the meaning changes according to whether *compte* is singular, plural, or part of the pronominal form.

## A pattern worth noticing

Past the everyday A2 meanings, *rendre* often appears in larger constructions:

- *se rendre compte de*
- *se rendre à*
- *rendre justice à*
- *rendre des comptes*

Recognising these complete patterns is much more useful than searching for another English translation of *rendre*.

## Summary

Beyond returning things, *rendre* becomes a verb of realisation, movement, responsibility, and fixed expressions. Learning these constructions as whole units will make authentic French much easier to understand at [B1](/en/cefr/).
