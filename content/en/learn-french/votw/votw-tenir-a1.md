# Tenir

*Tenir* is one of the most useful French verbs, although it can seem difficult at first because it has several meanings.

The closest English meaning is "to hold," but French also uses *tenir* for keeping promises and talking about the people and things that matter to you.

What you hold might be a bag in your hand, a promise you make, or an idea you care about. The verb also shows up in everyday combinations that do not translate word for word, such as keeping someone company or running a shop.

Learn these patterns and you will be able to use *tenir* in many everyday conversations.

## Forms and grammar

*Tenir* is irregular, and the stem changes as you move through the present tense:

<!-- table: forms -->

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| je tiens | nous tenons |
| tu tiens | vous tenez |
| il / elle tient | ils / elles tiennent |

The singular forms all begin with *tien-*, while the plural uses *ten-*. This pattern is shared by verbs such as *retenir* and *obtenir*.

In the past tense, *tenir* uses *avoir*, and the participle is *tenu*:

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| J'ai tenu la porte. | I held the door. |
| Nous avons tenu notre promesse. | We kept our promise. |

## How to use tenir

The three uses of *tenir* below are sorted by what is being "held": a physical object, something you keep, or something that matters to you.

### Holding something

This is the most literal sense, and the closest to English.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens mon sac. | I'm holding my bag. |
| Tiens la porte, s'il te plaît. | Hold the door, please. |
| Elle tient son bébé dans les bras. | She's holding her baby in her arms. |

### Keeping something

French also uses *tenir* for keeping a promise, keeping your word, or staying committed to something.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens ma promesse. | I keep my promise. |
| Nous tenons nos engagements. | We keep our commitments. |
| Il tient toujours sa parole. | He always keeps his word. |

### Caring about something: *tenir à*

One of the most common uses of *tenir* is *tenir à*, meaning "to care about" or "to attach importance to."

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens à ma famille. | I care about my family. |
| Nous tenons à nos amis. | We care about our friends. |
| Elle tient beaucoup à ce livre. | She cares a lot about this book. |

You will also hear *Je tiens à...* before making an important point. This use is a little more formal than everyday chat, but you will meet it early in polite speech and writing.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens à vous remercier. | I'd like to thank you. |
| Je tiens à être clair. | I want to be clear. |

Here, *tenir à* is closer to "I want to make a point of..." than simply "I care about."

## Other common constructions

Here are three more combinations using *tenir* that you will often hear, each a little less predictable from English:

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| tenir compagnie à quelqu'un | to keep someone company |
| tenir un restaurant | to run a restaurant |
| ça tient dans la poche | it fits in the pocket |

The first is worth a second look. In English, we *keep someone company*. French uses *tenir compagnie à quelqu'un*.

## Expressions and idioms

In fixed expressions *tenir* often has a meaning that is difficult to guess, so learn the whole phrase rather than translating each word.

### Tiens !

Used when giving something to someone.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Tiens, voilà ton téléphone. | Here, here's your phone. |

### tenir bon

To not give up.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Courage, tiens bon ! | Hang in there! |

## Common mistakes

One mistake comes from forgetting the preposition after *tenir*. Another comes from choosing the wrong "keep" verb in French.

### Forgetting *à*

When *tenir* means "to care about," it must be followed by *à*.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| *Je tiens ma famille.* | *Je tiens à ma famille.* |

### Confusing *tenir* and *garder*

Both verbs can sometimes mean "to keep," but they are not interchangeable. For a secret, French uses *garder*, not *tenir*.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| *Je tiens un secret.* | *Je garde un secret.* |

*Garder* usually means keeping or looking after something you have. *Tenir* is the usual choice for keeping a promise or your word: *Je tiens ma promesse.*

## Related verbs

- garder: to keep, to look after
- porter: to carry, to wear
- maintenir: to maintain
- retenir: to remember, to retain

> *Garder* and *tenir* overlap in English, but not in every situation. You *gardes* a secret or your keys, but you *tiens* a promise.

## Before you go

Learn *tenir* through the combinations where it appears rather than by translating it directly. At [A1](/en/cefr/) level, it will help you talk about everyday actions, promises, and the people and things that matter most.
