# Idioms and fixed expressions with *arriver*

At [A2](/en/cefr/), *arriver* covers arriving somewhere, managing to do something with *arriver à*, and talking about events that happen. Around [B1](/en/cefr/), the verb develops a handful of useful fixed constructions that are common in conversation and writing. Unlike verbs such as *faire* or *prendre*, these patterns are fewer in number, but they are frequent enough to deserve learning as complete expressions.

## Finally managing: *en arriver à*

Adding *en* changes the focus. Instead of simply managing to do something, *en arriver à* describes reaching a point after a process or series of events.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| J'en suis arrivé à vendre ma voiture. | I ended up selling my car. |
| Elle en est arrivée à ne plus lui parler. | She ended up not speaking to him anymore. |
| Comment en est-on arrivé là ? | How did we end up here? |

The emphasis is on the path that led to the result, not just the result itself.

## Expressions with *il arrive que*

At A2 you may have met *il arrive que...* meaning "sometimes...". At B1 it appears more naturally in longer sentences and formal writing.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Il arrive que les trains soient en retard. | Trains are sometimes late. |
| Il arrive qu'on change d'avis. | Sometimes people change their minds. |

This construction is more common in careful speech than in casual conversation.

## Reaching a conclusion

French uses *arriver à* with abstract nouns as well as infinitives.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| arriver à une conclusion | to reach a conclusion |
| arriver à un accord | to reach an agreement |
| arriver au pouvoir | to come to power |

English often changes the verb, while French keeps *arriver*.

## Fixed expressions

Several common expressions are easiest to learn as complete units.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| arriver à temps | to arrive on time |
| arriver trop tard | to arrive too late |
| ça m'arrive | it happens to me |

Although their meanings are transparent, these combinations occur so frequently that they become set patterns.

## A pattern worth noticing

Past the everyday A2 meanings, *arriver* usually becomes more abstract rather than more idiomatic.

Notice how the meaning shifts with the construction:

- *arriver à* + noun (reach an outcome)
- *en arriver à* (end up...)
- *il arrive que...* (it sometimes happens that...)
- *ça m'arrive* (it happens to me)

These are worth recognising as complete patterns instead of translating *arriver* each time.

## Summary

Compared with many high-frequency French verbs, *arriver* has relatively few advanced idioms. Its B1 uses mainly extend the core idea of reaching a destination into reaching a situation, conclusion, or outcome.
