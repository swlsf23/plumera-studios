# Tenir

*Tenir* is a useful, expressive verb that is worth learning in depth. Learners often start with the idea of "to hold," then discover the same stem covers keeping a promise and saying what matters to someone.

English splits those ideas across *hold*, *keep*, and *matter*. French often folds them into *tenir* instead. That mix of meanings is why the verb can feel confusing at first.

A suitcase in your hand, a door you hold open, a shop someone runs, a friend whose company you keep: *tenir* can show up in all of those. Some uses are literal. Others are fixed phrases where a word-for-word translation does not help.

Learn these patterns and you will be able to use *tenir* to express yourself in many everyday contexts.

## Forms and grammar

*Tenir* is irregular, and the stem changes for each form in the present tense:

<!-- table: forms -->

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| je tiens | nous tenons |
| tu tiens | vous tenez |
| il / elle tient | ils / elles tiennent |

Notice the split:

- Singular forms use *tien-* (*je tiens*, *tu tiens*, *il / elle tient*).
- Plural forms use *ten-* (*nous tenons*, *vous tenez*, *ils / elles tiennent*).

After getting used to the shape of *tenir*, verbs like *retenir* and *obtenir* become easier too.

In the past tense, *tenir* uses *avoir*, and the past participle is *tenu*:

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| J'ai tenu la porte. | I held the door. |
| Nous avons tenu notre promesse. | We kept our promise. |

## How to use tenir

The three uses below follow what *tenir* is doing: holding something physical, keeping a promise or your word, or marking what matters with *tenir à*.

### Holding something

This is the physical sense: an object in your hand, a door you hold open, or someone in your arms.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens mon sac. | I'm holding my bag. |
| Tiens la porte, s'il te plaît. | Hold the door, please. |
| Elle tient son bébé dans les bras. | She's holding her baby in her arms. |

### Keeping a promise or your word

When French talks about keeping a promise or keeping your word, *tenir* is the usual verb.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens ma promesse. | I keep my promise. |
| Nous tenons nos engagements. | We keep our commitments. |
| Il tient toujours sa parole. | He always keeps his word. |

### Caring about something: *tenir à*

Add *à* afterward, and *tenir* marks what matters to you.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens à ma famille. | I care about my family. |
| Nous tenons à nos amis. | We care about our friends. |
| Elle tient beaucoup à ce livre. | She cares a lot about this book. |

The same shape introduces something you want to stress. *Je tiens à...* is a little more formal than everyday conversation, and you will hear it in polite speech and writing.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Je tiens à vous remercier. | I especially want to thank you. |
| Je tiens à être clair. | I want to be clear. |

Here *tenir à* is closer to "I especially want to..." than "I care about."

## Other common constructions

Three more combinations you will hear often. None of them is a simple "hold" in the hand:

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| tenir compagnie à quelqu'un | to keep someone company |
| tenir un restaurant | to run a restaurant |
| ça tient dans la poche | it fits in the pocket |

The first is the one that trips English speakers. We *keep* someone company. French uses *tenir*. 

The restaurant example means you run the place, tu est le chef, le patron. 

*Ça tient dans la poche* is about fitting, not gripping.

## Expressions and idioms

In fixed expressions, learn the whole phrase. The meaning is hard to guess from *tenir* alone.

### Tiens !

Used when you hand something to someone.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Tiens, voilà ton téléphone. | Here, here's your phone. |

### tenir bon

To keep going when something is hard.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Courage, tiens bon ! | Keep going! Don't give up! |

## Common mistakes

One mistake drops the preposition after *tenir*. The other picks the wrong French verb for English "keep."

### Forgetting *à*

When *tenir* means "to care about," it needs *à*.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| *Je tiens ma famille.* | *Je tiens à ma famille.* |

### Confusing *tenir* and *garder*

English "keep" covers both. French does not. For a secret, use *garder*, not *tenir*.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| *Je tiens un secret.* | *Je garde un secret.* |

*Garder* is for keeping or looking after something you have. *Tenir* is the usual choice for a promise or your word: *Je tiens ma promesse.*

## Related verbs

- garder: to keep, to look after
- porter: to carry, to wear
- maintenir: to maintain
- retenir: to remember, to retain

> *Garder* is the one to watch next to *tenir*. For a secret or your keys, French uses *garder*: *je garde un secret*. For a promise, it uses *tenir*: *je tiens ma promesse*.

## Before you go

Treat *tenir* as a set of patterns, not a translation of a single English verb. At [A2](/en/cefr/), that pays off when you hold a door, keep a promise, or say what matters with *tenir à*.
