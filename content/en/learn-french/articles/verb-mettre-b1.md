# Idioms and fixed expressions with *mettre*

At [A2](/en/cefr/), *mettre* covers putting something somewhere, putting on clothes, and saying how long something takes. That core is covered in the [French Verb of the Week lesson on *mettre*](/en/learn-french/votw/votw-mettre-a2/).

Around [B1](/en/cefr/), you meet *mettre* in expressions where "put" no longer helps much. Some uses are pronominal. Others are fixed phrases for states, plans, blame, or social awkwardness. Learn them as whole patterns. The same shapes show up again and again.

## Starting, or getting going

French often uses *mettre* when an action begins, or when someone finally gets to work.

### Se mettre à + infinitive

To start doing something, often with a sense that the action begins suddenly or after a pause. At A2 you may already know the present. At B1 you will also hear past forms, and weather can "start" the same way.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Elle s'est mise à travailler. | She started working. |
| Il s'est mis à pleuvoir. | It started raining. |
| Nous nous sommes mis à chanter. | We started singing. |

Notice the past participle agreement with the subject when the reflexive is the direct object: *elle s'est mise*, *ils se sont mis*.

### S'y mettre and se remettre à

*S'y mettre* means getting down to a task you have been avoiding. *Se remettre à* means starting again after a break.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Allez, on s'y met. | Come on, let's get to it. |
| Elle s'y est mise tôt ce matin. | She got started on it early this morning. |
| Il s'est remis à courir. | He took up running again. |

## Entering a state: *se mettre en…*

With *en* + a noun, *se mettre* often marks entering an emotional or social state.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Il s'est mis en colère. | He got angry. |
| Nous nous sommes mis d'accord. | We reached an agreement. |
| Elle s'est mise en route. | She set off. |

English switches verbs (*got*, *reached*, *ended up*). French keeps *se mettre en…* and changes the noun.

## Social ease and awkwardness

These expressions describe how comfortable someone feels, or how clumsily something was said.

### Mettre à l'aise / mettre mal à l'aise

To make someone feel comfortable, or the opposite.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Son sourire m'a mis à l'aise. | His smile put me at ease. |
| Cette question m'a mis mal à l'aise. | That question made me uncomfortable. |

### Mettre les pieds dans le plat

To say something awkward or embarrassing without meaning to.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Il a encore mis les pieds dans le plat. | He put his foot in it again. |
| Ne mets pas les pieds dans le plat devant ses parents. | Don't put your foot in it in front of his parents. |

French pictures feet going into the dish. English keeps the foot and drops the dish. Learn the whole phrase.

## Emphasis, blame, and warning

These uses show up in conversation, news, and work talk. Something is highlighted, questioned, or flagged as risky.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Le rapport met l'accent sur la sécurité. | The report emphasizes safety. |
| On a mis en cause le directeur. | The director was called into question. |
| Je te mets en garde. | I'm warning you. |

*Mettre l'accent sur* highlights a point. *Mettre en cause* questions someone's role or responsibility. *Mettre en garde* warns before something goes wrong.

## Plans, delays, and practical setups

A second cluster keeps a thin link to "putting" something into a process or onto a later date.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Nous avons mis le projet au point. | We finalized / fine-tuned the project. |
| Ils ont mis en place un nouveau système. | They set up a new system. |
| On a remis la réunion à plus tard. | We postponed the meeting. |

*Mettre au point* is about getting the details right. *Mettre en place* sets something up so it can run. *Remettre à plus tard* pushes an action into the future.

## A pattern worth noticing

Past the core A2 meanings, *mettre* often depends on what follows:

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| *se mettre à* / *s'y mettre* / *se remettre à* | start, get to it, start again |
| *se mettre en…* | enter a state |
| *mettre à l'aise* / *mettre mal à l'aise* / *mettre les pieds dans le plat* | comfort and awkwardness |
| *mettre l'accent sur* / *mettre en cause* / *mettre en garde* | emphasis, blame, warning |
| *mettre au point* / *mettre en place* / *remettre à plus tard* | plans and setups |

These are better learned as complete expressions than as separate translations of "put."

## Summary

Beyond the everyday [A2](/en/cefr/) uses in the [VOTW lesson](/en/learn-french/votw/votw-mettre-a2/), *mettre* shows up in whole expressions for starting, states, social awkwardness, emphasis, and plans. Learn these as patterns, and you will recognize them more easily at [B1](/en/cefr/) and beyond.
