# Arriver - advanced

At [A2](/en/cefr/), *arriver* covers arriving somewhere, managing to do something with *arriver à*, and talking about events that happen. That core is covered in the [French Verb of the Week lesson on *arriver*](/en/learn-french/votw/arriver/basics-a2/).

Around [B1](/en/cefr/), *arriver* does not expand into dozens of colorful idioms the way some verbs do. Instead, a few constructions push the same idea of "reaching" into more abstract territory. Learn those patterns as wholes.

## Ending up somewhere: *en arriver à*

Adding *en* changes the focus. *Arriver à* can mean managing to do something. *En arriver à* describes reaching a point after a process, often with a sense of "ending up" there.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| J'en suis arrivé à vendre ma voiture. | I ended up selling my car. |
| Elle en est arrivée à ne plus lui parler. | She ended up not speaking to him anymore. |
| Comment en est-on arrivé là ? | How did we end up here? |

The path matters as much as the result. Something led to this point, even if the sentence does not spell out every step.

## Sometimes it happens: *il arrive que*

*Il arrive que* introduces something that happens from time to time. It is more common in careful speech and writing than in casual converstion, where *ça arrive* or *parfois* may feel more natural.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Il arrive que les trains soient en retard. | Trains are sometimes late. |
| Il arrive qu'on change d'avis. | Sometimes people change their minds. |
| Il arrive que je travaille le week-end. | Sometimes I work on weekends. |

After *il arrive que*, French often uses the subjunctive (*soient*, *change*). Treat the whole construction as a unit: *il arrive que* + clause.

## Reaching an abstract outcome: *arriver à* + noun

At A2, *arriver à* usually takes an infinitive. At B1 you will also meet it with abstract nouns: a conclusion, an agreement, a position.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Nous sommes arrivés à une conclusion. | We reached a conclusion. |
| Ils sont arrivés à un accord. | They reached an agreement. |
| Le parti est arrivé au pouvoir. | The party came to power. |

English often switches verbs (*reach*, *come to*). French keeps *arriver à* and changes the noun.

## When it happens to someone: *ça m'arrive*

*Ça arrive* is already useful at A2 as a general "it happens." With a pronoun, French can mark who experiences the event.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| Ça m'arrive souvent. | It happens to me often. |
| Ça lui est déjà arrivé. | It has already happened to him / her. |
| Ça nous arrive à tous. | It happens to all of us. |

The event is still the thing that "arrives." The pronoun shows who it reaches.

## A pattern worth noticing

Past the everyday [A2](/en/cefr/) meanings, *arriver* usually becomes more abstract rather than more idiomatic.

| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| *en arriver à* | end up (after a process) |
| *il arrive que* | it sometimes happens that |
| *arriver à* + noun | reach an abstract outcome |
| *ça m'arrive* | it happens to me |

These are worth recognizing as complete patterns instead of translating *arriver* each time.

## Summary

Compared with many high-frequency French verbs, *arriver* has relatively few advanced idioms. Its [B1](/en/cefr/) uses mainly extend the core idea of reaching a destination into reaching a situation, conclusion, or outcome. Learn those constructions, and the verb will feel much more predictable beyond the [A2](/en/cefr/) lesson.
